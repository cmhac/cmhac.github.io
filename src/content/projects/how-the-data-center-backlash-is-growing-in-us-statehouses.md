As AI companies rushed to build the server farms that power their platforms, state lawmakers across the country and across the political spectrum were increasingly racing to limit them. Working with Cole Reynolds and Klara Auerbach, I set out to measure that race directly rather than anecdotally, assembling an analysis of legislative records to count how statehouses were actually responding.

We identified at least 375 data center bills introduced in 2026 — and found that roughly three-quarters of them sought to restrict development rather than encourage it. The opposition crossed party lines, driven largely by rising electricity costs and the environmental toll in the communities where these facilities get built.

Working at the level of individual bills, rather than tracking the handful of fights that had already made national news, let us show where the conflict was furthest along. Illinois, Virginia and Pennsylvania were among the states moving toward tighter regulation.
