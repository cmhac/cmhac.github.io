In the final weeks before the midterms, I wanted to measure something that is easy to feel and hard to count: how much of the conversation among Trump's core supporters is priming them to doubt the result. I wrote the story with Laura Meckler and Patrick Marley, and the analysis behind it.

I used a large language model to sort social media posts, podcast transcripts and other online statements from high-profile politicians, pundits, podcasters and influencers into election-security topics, and combined that with Factbase data on Trump's own remarks, speeches and press conferences. The classifier had an accuracy rate of over 97 percent on a human-labeled test set of more than 200 statements.

That turned up more than 7,000 statements this year from nearly 600 right-wing accounts, podcasts and other outlets questioning the election's integrity. Claims that noncitizens are on voter rolls and that mail-in ballots enable widespread fraud were twice as prevalent as during the 2024 cycle, while talk of voting machines, a staple of past elections, has largely been supplanted. Trump himself made nearly 300 such statements in his second term, and mentioned voter ID and mail-in ballots six times more often in 2026 than in 2025.

I ran the same analysis on the left. Statements pushing back on these narratives more than doubled compared with the 2024 cycle, a faster increase than in right-wing election integrity claims overall.
